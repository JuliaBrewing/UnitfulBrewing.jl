References

The sources of the definitions of the units and of the relations used in the equivalences. Where no original source was found, this is said explicitly.

Volumes

  • US units (gal = 231 in³, qt, pt, floz, cup, tbsp, tsp): NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C, "General Tables of Units of Measurement", https://www.nist.gov/document/appc-19-hb44-finaldocx.
  • US beer barrel (bbl = 31 gal): 27 CFR § 25.11, "Meaning of terms" (Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/27/25.11; see also the TTB beer FAQ, https://www.ttb.gov/beer/beer-faqs.
  • Imperial units (igal = 4.54609 L, iqt, ipt, gi, ifloz): UK Weights and Measures Act 1985, Schedule 1, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/72/schedule/1. The imperial beer barrel (ibbl) of 36 imperial gallons is the traditional British one.

Sugar contents and gravity

  • Degrees Plato, Brix and Balling are mass percentages of sucrose (or extract) in solution, from the tables of Balling (1843), Brix (1854) and Plato (early 20th century). The tables differ slightly; they are taken as equal here.
  • Gravity units (gu = 1000 (sg − 1)) and gravity points are the usual brewing conventions.
  • SugarGravity, °P = 259 (1 − 1/sg): given by T. Foster, "Understanding Specific Gravity vs. Plato", Brew Your Own, https://byo.com/?p=14253, as one of the simpler of several proposed relations. No original source was found.
  • SugarGravityQuad, °P = 668.72 sg − 463.37 − 205.35 sg²: attributed to Robbins (1975) in "On the relationship between Plato and specific gravity", Brewer's Friend, 2012, https://www.brewersfriend.com/2012/10/31/on-the-relationship-between-plato-and-specific-gravity. The original publication was not found.

Color

  • SRM and EBC: both are proportional to the absorbance at 430 nm, A430, in a 1 cm cell. SRM = 12.7 A430 (ASBC Methods of Analysis, Beer-10, adopted in 1950) and EBC = 25 A430 (EBC Analytica), hence EBC = (25/12.7) SRM ≈ 1.97 SRM. See e.g. the Thermo Fisher application note "Beer analysis", https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/MSD/Application-Notes/SN53076-analyze-beer-color-uv-vis.pdf, and the ASBC's own pages on color.
  • Lovibond, SRM = 1.3546 °L − 0.76: an empirical fit to the EBC and Lovibond values published by maltsters, as described in the Wikipedia article "Standard Reference Method", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Reference_Method. Its original source was not found. The fit is good for pale colors and worsens for dark ones.

Diastatic power

  • Windisch–Kolbach, °WK = 3.5 °Lintner − 16: the usual approximate interconversion between the ASBC (°Lintner) and EBC (°WK) methods. It is given, e.g., in G. Oliver (ed.), The Oxford Companion to Beer, Oxford University Press, 2011, and in the Megazyme data sheet E-MASTP, https://prod-docs.megazyme.com/documents/Data_Sheet/E-MASTP_DATA.pdf.

Carbonation

  • vols: volumes of CO₂, at 0 °C and 1 atm, per volume of beer, as in the BeerJSON schema, https://github.com/beerjson/beerjson.
  • CarbonationDensity: uses the density of gaseous CO₂ at 0 °C and 1 atm, 1.977 kg/m³ (CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics), which follows from its molar mass of 44.009 g/mol.

Concentrations

  • DensityConcentration, 1 ppm ≈ 1 mg/L: this assumes a density of 1 kg/L for the solution. It is the usual convention for dilute aqueous solutions, as in brewing water chemistry.
